# Interview notes (from the two recordings)

Source: `Jb website 1.m4a` (about 6 minutes) and `Jb website 2.m4a` (about 25 minutes). I transcribed them with an automatic speech-to-text tool. The audio is noisy, so **names, numbers and dates below may be wrong** until the owner or founder confirms them. The raw transcripts are deliberately **not** in this repository: they contain stories about other private people. Keep the recordings and transcripts private.

Confidence key: **Clear** = said plainly and consistently. **Check** = transcription was unclear or the claim is a big public statement. Nothing marked Check should appear on the live site until the owner confirms it.

## Founder's boxing story

- Started boxing at about 15, one junior fight, then joined Finchley Boxing Club for the best part of three years. **Clear**
- Finchley had internal disagreements; he and his brothers moved to Tottenham New Enterprise Boxing Club (long trip from Highgate). Described Tottenham as a hard, serious gym, "like going to the Bronx". **Clear**
- Family name is Paul. Older brother Steve (middleweight, very talented, less disciplined), younger brother John (very fast hands). The founder says both were better boxers than him as amateurs and that his own strength was power punching. **Clear**
- Quote used on the demo site, recording 1: "it breaks you down to build you up" (full line: "things that make you the person you are, because it breaks you down to build you up"). **Clear** in the audio; founder to approve before launch.
- A Finchley trainer told the brothers they would be fighters and told the founder to go and stick to the heavy bag. It made him hungrier; he later knocked opponents out early in amateur fights. **Clear** (trainer's name omitted on purpose)
- After about six fights he was entered in the ABAs (national amateur championships), age 16. How that first ABA bout ended was cut off in recording 1. Recording 2 says he reached ABA semi-finals/finals and won some titles. **Check**
- Shoulder dislocations at about 17; operation at 17 to 18; out of boxing from 18 to 24 but kept training. **Check** (ages)
- Turned professional at about 25 after a trial, signed to Barry Hearn's stable. He was known as "Bernie" in boxing. **Check** (name spellings)
- Won the Southern Area title and held it about five years; later won the Commonwealth title; says he was about 30 by then. Early pro wins were very quick knockouts. **Check** (these are public claims; confirm exact titles, years and wording before use)
- He says his day job at the time (a Royal Mail postman, known locally for it) meant promoters were slow to give him title fights. **Check**

## How the gym came about

- He now works in facilities for Proposition Studios. He was offered the job after being overheard at a party; two years at the Camden site, then Bethnal Green opposite York Hall. **Clear**
- The chairman showed the team a dark, grubby basement and said to turn it into a gym. It has since been transformed with posters and donated equipment (bags and gloves donated, some by artists). **Clear**
- Barry Hearn, his former promoter, arranged a boxing ring after a phone call and text; the canvas carries the Bethnal Green Boxing Club name. **Check** (do not publish the Barry Hearn link without his or the founder's say-so)
- At the time of recording: five classes held so far, two sessions a week (Tuesday and Thursday), personal trainers and the film industry interested in using the space. **Clear**

## Prices heard (not yet confirmed as final)

- Per class: £5 for Proposition artists, £10 for everyone else. Matches the repo.
- Monthly subscription mentioned: £25 for Proposition artists, £40 for others, "use it as many as you want". **New, not in the prototype. Confirm before adding.**
- "It's not all about money, it's about giving something back." Good tone anchor.

## Community and values

- Before the gym he ran a small training group in his converted garage in Cockfosters during COVID; some young people from difficult situations trained there and, he says, turned their lives around. Do not publish details of these young people. **Clear**
- Themes: giving back, mentoring and discipline, a father-figure style of support for young people who lack one, belonging to a club, group photo at the end of sessions.
- Wants to find local funders so the club can run things such as a minibus for day outs for young people and adults who need a break, and wants the council involved. These are goals, not current services.

## Website wishes

- Look and feel: **rustic, authentic, old-school** like the gym (old posters and magazines), not a fancy mirror-and-treadmill gym. Light or dark was left to the designer.
- Features asked for: class booking where classes can be created; print-on-demand merchandise (t-shirts, shorts, hand wraps, gloves) with prepaid orders; a community page (local landmarks, Bethnal Green history, York Hall, the council and supporters); space for personal trainers and filming hire.
- Logo and existing merchandise artwork are still to come from the owner.
